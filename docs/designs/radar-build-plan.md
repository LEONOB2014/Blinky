## Problem Statement

30X (Reto "Radar de contenido") wants to know, reliably and cheaply, which AI topics are about to take
off on LinkedIn, why, whether a trend is starting or already saturated, and whether it fits LatAm
executives learning AI. LinkedIn cannot be collected automatically, so the radar measures the open
upstream chain (GDELT news with tone and GCAM emotions, Hacker News, Wikipedia es/pt/en, optionally X counts,
YouTube) and outputs a weekly Spanish top 10 with reasons, stage, emotional angle and an adaptation
brief. The product plan is `docs/plan/MASTER_PLAN.pdf`; this document settles how it is built in the
16 hours left (Sat 19:30 to Sun 12:00, UTC-5) by six coding-agent accounts, and the demo order.

## What Makes This Cool

- The judges are on the business side. A live, GPU-rendered temporal graph of 90 days of AI news,
  with communities forming and changing emotional colour, is the moment they remember (user).
- Every card answers a business question: "what should 30X post this week, in which emotional
  angle, and how much time is left before it lands in Spanish" (the "window ~N days" chip).
- It is ToS-clean by construction: it never touches LinkedIn, and it says so.

## Constraints

**The GDELT loop (D1, WS1 + WS2, repeated until the checks pass).** Extract (S2) → aggregate (S3, S4,
S8) → segment into the three rings → check (row counts against `daily_totals`, `is_missing` days NULL,
per-segment selectivity, regex-vs-label agreement) → adjust the rules → re-run on the dev slice. The
wide net costs no extra bytes in BigQuery because bytes depend on columns, not rows (plan §8), so
widening to rings 2 and 3 changes rules, not cost. Ring definitions: ring 1 = the plan's core-AI rule;
ring 2 = World Bank ICT themes or a tech-organization list, minus ring 1; ring 3 = a domain theme
group (business and economy, climate and energy, labour, health, education, elections and
misinformation, law and regulation, security; GKG theme prefixes listed in `taxonomy.yaml`)
co-occurring with a tech organization, minus rings 1–2. The contracts carry `segment` (`ai`, `it`,
`ai_domain`) and, for ring 3, `domain`.

Ring rules, without overlap: an article is ring 1 if and only if it passes the core-AI rule. Subtopics
(12, multi-label) are assigned to ring-1 articles only; `energy_climate` has its own rule in
`taxonomy.yaml` (GKG `ENV_*` and energy themes, plus a data-centre power and water lexicon) applied
inside ring 1. A climate article that fails the core-AI rule can only be ring 3, domain `climate`, and is
context, never ranked. Domain to subtopic map, for the "why" bullets: business → `business_adoption`,
`funding_markets`; labour → `jobs_work`; health → `health_science`; education → `education`; law →
`regulation_policy`; security and elections/misinformation → `safety_risk`; climate → `energy_climate`.

**What is ranked.** Not the 12 subtopics (a top 10 of 12 would equal the base rate). The ranked unit is a
ring-1 *topic*: a named entity (organization, product, model) or a GKG theme inside ring 1 with ≥ 20
articles in the last 7 days, resolved to Wikidata so its es/pt Wikipedia articles are known. Leiden
communities are not ranked: they are context on the card (the community a topic belongs to) and appear in
the graph view. Expected universe per week: 150–400 topics (estimate; the measured PoC had 10,487 entities from
6,544 AI docs). Each topic carries its subtopic(s). The per-topic series lives in a gold table `topic_daily`
(`topic_id`, `dt`, `lang_bucket`, `segment`, `n_articles`, `share_of_day`, tone statistics), built in
BigQuery by WS1 (Claude #2) from `radar.gkg_ai` (its `orgs` and `themes` columns, which S2 already
extracts) over the whole backtest span. It reads our own extracted table, not the GDELT source, so its
scan is a small part of the budget; its bytes are logged like every other job.

**1-hop context ring (P6).** For each ranked topic: its neighbours in `edges_weekly` inside ring-1
articles with n ≥ 3 and NPMI > 0.1, dropping hub nodes (the top 1% by weighted degree that week), top 20
by NPMI. It costs no extra BigQuery bytes (the edges come from articles already extracted). It feeds the
"why" bullets and the card's context line. Two-hop neighbourhoods are computed only for the graph view in
G, capped at 80 nodes per topic (plan §11.1).

**Gate for G (strict).** G starts only when all of these pass on full data: the cards page renders the
full-data top 10 with every chip, the backtest has a result (whatever it is), and the filter P/R card
has its numbers. No graph visualization work happens before the gate. Until then, Claude #4 computes the
graph metrics that feed Emergence and the 1-hop ring (degree growth, new-neighbour rate, Leiden), which
are not visualization. Earliest feasible gate: about 06:00 (full-data scores by 05:00, filter labels
verified 04:00–05:30). Thresholds come from the ~3 h Graphistry estimate and the 10:00 freeze: gate
passed by 06:30 → full G (Graphistry temporal view, story view, video); passed between 06:30 and 07:00 →
Graphistry temporal view and its recorded video only, no story view; not passed by 07:00 → no live graph,
and the demo closes on the cards. This is the risk of the strict gate, accepted by the user ("only when
everything is working"). The Graphistry path is
untested before G (estimate: about 3 hours), so the recorded video is the fallback for any Graphistry
failure.

### Evaluation (what every number on the page means)

**Backtest (WS4, Claude #3).** Unit: topic-week over the ranked universe above (150–400 topics a
week, estimate). In the backtest, the radar and both baselines rank only topics with an es or pt Wikipedia
article (top 10 among mapped topics); unmapped topics are counted and printed. The live radar ranks all
topics and marks the unmapped ones. Positive label:
the topic's es+pt pageview growth over the next 2 weeks is in the top quartile of that week's topics.
Precision@10 per week = positives among the radar's top 10 (base rate 25% by construction, printed next
to it). Baselines on the same weeks: raw-count growth and persistence (last week's top 10). Lead time per
flagged positive = weeks from the first flag to the pageview peak within the next 8 weeks.
Data span: the backtest uses its own span, separate from the 90-day graph window. GDELT gold aggregates:
backfill target 12 months, minimum 6 months (plan cut line); pageviews: the same span (the Wikimedia API
is free and goes back years). Evaluable weeks = span − 5 warm-up weeks − 2 label weeks for precision (−8
for lead time), minus weeks overlapping the 2025-06-14..07-02 GDELT gap: about 45 / 39 weeks with 12
months, 19 / 13 with 6 months. Below 10 evaluable weeks the page prints the counts without an interval.
The count of weeks and topics is printed with every result. Reporting: precision@10 as positives/total
with a bootstrap interval over weeks; lead time as a median with its interval. If the radar does not
beat the baselines, the page says so and shows the diagnosis.

**Filter P/R (WS2, Claude #2).** `f_control` is a 0.2% random sample of all GKG rows (plan §8); the
core-AI lexicon selects 1.41% of rows (measured, plan §8), so a purely random sample of 200 would hold about
3 AI articles, too few for recall. Ground truth therefore has two parts: 150 stratified ring-1 candidates
(by language and subtopic) for precision, and 150 control rows enriched by a weak signal independent of the
core-AI rule (an ICT theme or a tech organization) for recall: 75 rows the core-AI rule accepted and 75 it
rejected, both drawn from the tech-themed stratum of `f_control`. Estimator: recall = N_acc·p_acc /
(N_acc·p_acc + N_rej·p_rej), where N_acc and N_rej are the counts of tech-themed control rows accepted and
rejected by the rule and p_acc, p_rej the AI shares found in the two labelled sets; the interval comes from
a bootstrap over the labelled rows. Reported as "recall among tech-themed news", with that scope stated.
The LLM pre-labels; one verifier, the user, checks all 300 rows on 04:00–05:30 (about 18 s per row).
Precision is reported as counts with Wilson intervals.

**Editor test (WS4 prepares the sheet by 08:30).** Raters: every available human (target 2: the user
and one other person). Items: radar top 10 and raw-count top 10 for 2026-W42; a topic on both lists is
shown once and left out of the comparison (the overlap count is reported). Order randomized with a fixed
seed, source hidden. Question: "would you publish on this topic this week?" yes/no. Report yes counts per
list with denominators (for example 5/7 vs. 2/7 when 3 topics overlap), each rater separately, with a small-n caveat on the
page.

**Window chip (WS4).** Each topic uses its primary subtopic (the one with the most of its articles over
the last 28 days). Typical lag L = the median, pooled over the topics with the same primary subtopic in the
history, of the days between a topic's English-bucket onset and its es/pt-bucket onset (onset = first day
`z_7d` > 2). Time left N = L − days since this topic's English onset in the current cycle. States: "~N días
restantes" when N > 0 (estimate, labelled so); "en cualquier momento" when N ≤ 0 and no es/pt onset yet;
"ya en español" when the es/pt onset has already happened; "sin estimación" with fewer than 3 historical
onsets for the subtopic or no English onset yet. Acceptance: a unit test on the dev slice covers all
four states, using real dev-slice topics; where the dev slice has no real case for a state, that
state's test lowers the 3-onset threshold, and the test says so.

**Hook (P4).** If the measured median lead time is at least 1 week with an interval above zero: "La
radar marca temas N semanas antes de que crezca la atención en Wikipedia en español (medido, n semanas)".
Otherwise: "Qué tema de IA está subiendo esta semana, por qué y desde qué ángulo contarlo, solo con
fuentes públicas".

### Mocks, dev slices and the release check

- **Mocks are never invented values.** Each `mocks/<name>.json` is a handful of rows copied from the real
  dev slice, so schema tests and UI work use real shapes and real values. Mocks follow the same allow-list
  as the fixtures: evidence fields hold the source domain and `gkg_id`, never a URL or title.
- **Dev slices (all real):** GDELT gold aggregates for 8 weeks (all three segments) and a 7-day `gkg_ai`
  sample (orchestrator, D0); Wikipedia pageviews for the mapped articles, 8 weeks, and HN counts (Grok, by
  22:00, a dependency of the WS4 brief); YouTube exemplar metadata for 20 videos (Grok, by 01:00).
- **Column allow-list for anything committed:** ids, dates, source domain, language, the seven V2Tone
  fields, word count, flags, themes, segment and topic labels, and organization names. Never: URL, title,
  persons, all-names, quotations, locations or any free-text field. A test fails if `fixtures/dev/`
  contains any column outside the allow-list.
- **Profiles:** `RADAR_PROFILE=dev|full` in `radar.paths`; every exported JSON carries
  `"_meta": {"profile": ...}`. The internal preview page (Gemini, end of D1) shows a "datos de desarrollo"
  banner and stays non-public on Netlify.
- **Evidence (headlines and URLs of public news) never enters git.** It lives only in the lake and in the
  deployed site. `web/public/data/` is git-ignored: `export_static.py` writes it at the freeze from the full
  lake and the site is deployed from that directory with `netlify deploy --dir`. `export_static.py` refuses
  to run unless `RADAR_PROFILE=full`; a unit test in CI checks that refusal and that the web build reads no
  file from `mocks/`.

## Reviewer Concerns

### R3-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The latest start for G does not fit the design's own estimate. The Graphistry path is estimated at about 3 hours, and the reductions only cut the story view, never the Graphistry temporal view or its video. A gate passed at 07:30 gives Graphistry until about 10:30; a gate at 08:29 gives until about 11:30. Both are after the 09:30 target and the 10:00 freeze. The recorded video cannot be the fallback, because it comes from the same Graphistry path. Even with full G, a gate after about 06:30 leaves less than 3 hours before 09:30. This is the time-budget part of R2-1, still unmet.

**Remedy**

> Derive the thresholds from the estimate and the freeze. For example: full G only if the gate passes by about 06:30; Graphistry view and video only (no story view) if it passes by about 07:00; no live graph after that. Or shorten the Graphistry estimate with a D1 smoke test (see R3-12) and recompute the thresholds.

### R3-2 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> A ranked topic can be a Leiden community, but the design says every topic is 'resolved to Wikidata so its es/pt Wikipedia articles are known'. A community is a set of entities, not a Wikidata item, so its label (pageview growth), its onset history for the window chip, and its lead time are undefined. Leiden communities are also recomputed each week, with no rule that tracks the same community from week to week. The topic-week backtest, 'last week's top 10' persistence and 'weeks from the first flag' all need a stable identity across weeks.

**Remedy**

> Either rank only named entities and themes (communities as context or graph view only), or define a community's identity across weeks (for example matching by member overlap) and how its pageview label is built (for example the sum over the mapped articles of its top members). State this before WS4 and WS6 are briefed.

### R3-3 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The ranked unit is now an entity or theme topic, but the backtest is said to run on 'GDELT gold aggregates' over 6 to 12 months. The design never says that these aggregates hold daily counts per entity and theme for ring-1 articles (the listed aggregates S3, S4 and S8 and the subtopic series suggest subtopic-level counts). Without per-topic history over the whole span, there is no topic-week ranking, no raw-count baseline and no historical onsets for the window chip. Producing it needs the organizations and themes columns for 12 months of ring-1 rows, with a byte cost that is not stated (see R3-14).

**Remedy**

> Name the gold table that holds per-topic daily counts (topic id, date, language bucket, segment) over the backtest span, its owner and its source columns, and include its scan in the backfill byte estimate.

### R3-4 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The recall sample contains only articles that the core-AI rule rejected (no AI lexicon hit), so it can find false negatives but no true positives. Recall among tech-themed news = true AI articles accepted / all true AI articles in that stratum, which needs the size of the accepted and rejected tech-themed strata and the true-AI rate in each. The precision sample is stratified by language and subtopic, not by tech theme, so it does not supply the accepted side directly. The design states no estimator, and the correction asked for in R2-5 is still missing. The expected number of true AI articles among the 150 rows is not given either.

**Remedy**

> State the estimator: for example recall = N_acc * p_acc / (N_acc * p_acc + N_rej * p_rej), with N_acc and N_rej the counts of tech-themed rows accepted and rejected by the rule, p_acc from the precision labels restricted to tech-themed rows, and p_rej from the 150 rows. Give its interval method (bootstrap or delta method rather than a single Wilson interval) and the expected false-negative count.

### R3-5 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**
